What the backtest actually tests

104-week rolling estimation; next-week realized returns; weekly rebalancing; unit gross exposure. Hyperparameters selected on the first half of the backtest, evaluated on the second half.

Why simplified robust shrinkage is absent

Multiplying all naive weights by a positive constant has no effect after unit-gross normalization. Including it as a distinct strategy would duplicate the naive portfolio.

Limitations to discuss in an interview

Full-sample universe screening can leak future information. The surviving-asset universe introduces survivorship bias and limits generalization. This does not establish why equal-weight outperformed; equal-weight remains exposed to market losses and asset-selection bias. Shorting costs, funding, liquidity limits and position constraints are omitted. Costs are fixed, and historical means may not persist across regimes.

Turnover and the cash convention

The notebook drifts risky weights using 1 + portfolio return and assumes implicit cash financing at zero. Non-unit net exposure and short positions require a more explicit cash and financing account for a production model.
